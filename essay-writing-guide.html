The Essay Writing Guide

How competent essays lose marks, and the handful of habits that stop it happening.

Read the questionArgument, not summaryPlanningParagraphsUsing sourcesEditing down

Most disappointing essay marks are not given for bad writing. They are given for well-written answers to a question slightly different from the one that was set.

Read the question, then read it again

Find the command word and treat it as an instruction, because it is one. “Discuss” wants a weighing-up. “Assess” wants a verdict with reasons. “To what extent” wants a position on a scale, not a yes or a no. “Compare” wants both things in every paragraph, not one and then the other.

Then find the limits: the period, the place, the case, the theory named. Everything outside them is unmarked work, however good it is.

An argument, not a summary

You should be able to state your answer in one sentence before you start writing. If the one-sentence version is a list of what various people think, you have a summary rather than an argument, and summaries sit in the middle band however neat they are.

That sentence goes near the top of the essay, not saved for the conclusion. Markers are reading to find out what you think; making them wait for it costs you.

Planning in ten minutes

Write the one-sentence answer. Under it, write the three or four claims that have to be true for it to hold. Under each claim, note the evidence and the strongest objection. That is the essay, and the planning took ten minutes.

Paragraphs a marker can follow

One claim per paragraph, stated in the first sentence. Evidence next. Then the part most people skip: what the evidence shows, in your words, and why it supports the claim. Finish by connecting back to the question rather than trailing off into the next topic.

Using sources rather than listing them

A source is used when you do something with it: agree and extend, disagree and say why, or set it against another. A source is listed when it appears in brackets and nothing follows. Eight sources genuinely used will beat thirty mentioned every time.

Quote sparingly and never to make a point you could make yourself. When the reference itself needs formatting, the Citation Formatter gives both the list entry and the in-text form in four styles.

Editing down

Write long, then cut. The first thing to go is the paragraph that restates the question. Then every sentence that announces what you are about to do. Then the adverbs. Most essays lose fifteen per cent of their words and nothing else in a single pass.

Read the final version against the question one last time, paragraph by paragraph, asking only: does this earn its place? Anything that does not is costing you marks by taking up words that could have gone elsewhere.
